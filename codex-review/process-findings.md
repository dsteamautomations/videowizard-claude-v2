# VideoWizard v2 — filmmaking process review

**1. Owner summary**

The next film is most exposed when an approved plan becomes a picture or moving shot.
Some added instructions contradict the plan: a worker cannot hold two tools, an object cannot leave the picture, and a planned look at the camera fails a check.
The drawing assistant also misses the approved master picture of an object.
Fix those handoffs and contradictory sentences first; they affect what the audience sees and cause paid remakes.
The second look at the first cut tells its helpers to discard some footage that the main instructions allow.
A corrected drawing can be judged against pictures left over from its previous version.
There are shorter fixes: pass the right approved pictures, delete conflicting words, and give each helper only the material it needs.
The price preview understates video with fractional seconds; the available prices do not establish the whole film’s cost.
This report reads the current files; it does not claim that these failures were observed in a live film.

**P10 — calls, money and waiting**

The code defines calls per item, not a “typical film.” Use **C** clips, **S** scenes, **V** video shots, **E** exact-end pairs, **P** generated pictures including candidates and references, **D** drawn shots. Additional remakes are extra calls, not free retries. Prices below are source defaults, not live billing.

- Intake, brief, script, treatment, planning and prompt writing: operator-side Claude work; the server makes no automatic generation call for these. No Claude price is configured. Scene review adds **S helper tasks**; gap planning adds roughly one helper per three/four gaps, with conversation turns not fixed by the code.
- Voice: **one provider call per tryout**, and **one per narration piece** per requested version. Saved tryouts are reused. The procedure caps tryouts at six and full narrations at three; a long script may use multiple pieces. No narration rupee/character price is in `config.py`. Source: `voices.py:487,509`; `worker.py:92–125`; voice steps.
- Analysis: **C provider studies** before retries; clips above 15 seconds use the agentic study and shorter clips the plain study. Policy-only reprocessing uses **zero new studies**. No Flash token price is configured. Source: `analysis_jobs.py:106,150,417`; analysis step 0.
- Optional options inspection: **one call per requested boundary**, otherwise zero; free boundary frames are not provider calls (S2:360,386).
- Specifications: **one measurement call per question**, if needed; **one wrong-look call per element when built**, not on every image. Approval of individual material specs starts it if missing; bulk approval currently misses it (finding 6). Background-person comparison adds **one call per qualifying picture approval with an earlier picture in its scene** (V:1035,1246,2667,2792).
- Pictures: **P generation calls + normally P checking calls**. Two start candidates mean two generation/check pairs. A requested fix adds one generation and one check; there is no automatic endless correction loop (`V:2442,3000`; `visual_workers.py:293,644`). Gemini output base is **₹11.792 per 1K/2K image**, **₹21.12 per 4K image**, plus **₹0.0968 per input picture**. These are `$0.134 / $0.24 / $0.0011 × ₹88` (`config.py:24–26,49`). OpenAI’s configured fallback/estimate is **₹4/image**; retained usage is priced by tokens, so ₹4 is not a verified unit bill (`V:344,366`).
- Video: **V generation calls**, plus **V take-check calls**; **E pair checks** when there are planned changes and an end image, cached for unchanged pairs. Each generated second is priced at **₹8.90**. Default generated length is the shot length rounded **up** to a whole second, at least three seconds (`config.py:48`; V:3524,3578; `visual_workers.py:411`). The procedure’s “about ₹1–2 a take” checking statement is not backed by a corresponding configurable checker price; it is not added as a verified cost.
- HyperFrames drawing: **D rules-helper tasks + D builder tasks + D fresh-checker tasks** before corrections; a failed shot adds a builder/checker pair per redo, at most two rounds. No server image/video-generation charge for the render itself. Claude costs and execution time are not configured. SVG alternative: one check, then up to two fix/check pairs: **one to five Gemini calls per drawing**, from the existing three-pass loop (`visual_workers.py:471–496`).
- Assembly and delivery: **zero generative provider calls** in the ordinary path. Rendering and operator review still take time.

**Three largest priced making steps in a worked small film:** assume 10 four-second video shots, two exact ends, two start candidates per shot, six photographic stills and four drawn shots. Four recurring elements—two people and two objects—need four sheets, two object golden pictures and one style picture. Assume no shared boundary, remakes or optional inspections. Then: **(1) video takes: 10 calls, ₹356; (2) shot pictures: 28 calls, ₹330.18 output base; (3) reference/style pictures: 7 calls, ₹82.54 output base**. Each picture also has its check; add parent-picture charges. The priced subtotal is **₹768.72**, excluding narration, analysis, checking and Claude. This is a transparent illustration, **not** a measured typical-film bill or proof of the three largest total costs.

**Time ranking limit:** current files do not establish the three longest provider stages for a completed film. The three timed provider steps in the previously recorded VW-12 material are **analysis: 13½ minutes for 46 clips; full narration: one minute; two concurrent tryouts: 20 seconds** (`design/126-flow-review-vw12.md:582,611,720–721`). Those are historical readings, not current measurements; no comparable completed visuals timing is supplied, so excluding visuals and calling these the overall top three would be misleading. That note also records 26 minutes from approved scenes to first-cut proposal and 16 minutes of shot answers, which include human/assistant work and are not provider latency (`:963,973`).

**Serial and overlapping work read:** tryouts are submitted together (`vw.py:1250–1255`); clip studies, image jobs and takes already run through multiple workers; motion builders are expressly batched. Generation before checking, start approval before an exact end, and successive graphic states have dependencies. Separate exact-end pair comparisons are independent across shots, but each runs synchronously inside `prepare_take` (`V:3578–3583`), so preparing shots one after another adds their waits. Batch picture approvals also run in order (`vw.py:1812,2070–2080`); background-person comparisons across different scenes are independent, while comparisons within one scene use earlier approved pictures. These identify possible overlap, not measured savings. No new parallel-work machinery is proposed: design/35:18 leaves the broader parallel-work proposals for discussion, and reading alone does not justify it here.
