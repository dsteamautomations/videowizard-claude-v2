# Styled stills brought to life

For a beat made entirely by the picture model and the video model, with no footage and no presenter: a styled still per beat, then a take that makes it breathe. Read with `playbooks/video-frame.md` and `playbooks/video-clip.md`.

## General: every category

### The principle

The still carries the idea: one literal, slightly surreal picture per beat. The take only makes it breathe; never ask the take to tell the story. This is an animated poster, not a filmed scene.

### The steps

1. **One still per beat**, in one of the styles below. One style per film, so every beat reads as one series; the look comes from the approved style picture.
2. **One take per still.** Motion belongs to the objects already in the frame; the composition never changes. The plan sets the seconds and any camera move.

### The motion brief for a styled still

Gentle, living motion of the existing elements only. One literal motion tied to the idea (the pile swells; the arrow creeps higher; the megaphone trembles), and at most one quiet motion around it (accents drift). Every element visible now is the only thing that ever appears; the composition stays exactly as it is; everything stays {the style: a flat printed collage, flat 2D vector, cut paper, a printed comic, handmade clay}.

### What goes wrong

- **Hands appear from nowhere** on pressing or handling motions, and writing "no hands" makes it worse. Never mention hands; give the motion to the objects and say the composition stays exactly as it is.
- **The last two seconds drift**: objects intrude, the style turns realistic. Check them on every take; trim before them or make the take again from the still.
- **One motion per beat.** Two motions read as chaos.

### Writing every style formula

- Words on a label, band or caption go only in `exact_text`, with their place and size; the prompt names only the shape that carries them and where it sits, by a landmark ("a torn-paper band just below the subject").
- Describe what the style is ("completely flat 2D"), not a list of what it is not.
- {Slots} are filled from the brief and the approved style picture.

## Picture kinds: styles that carry their own palette

- **Screen-print collage.** Flat screen-print collage poster: one saturated {colour} ground with fine newsprint grain; a grayscale halftone cutout of {the subject doing the idea}, treated as a paper sticker with a thin white die-cut edge, slightly torn, a soft shadow; two to four flat accent shapes (a cream circle, a black zigzag, scattered dots); a torn-paper label band just below the subject, in bold condensed newspaper capitals. Matte risograph print, limited palette, flat print only.
- **Flat vector explainer.** A friendly simplified {subject} in bold flat shapes with rounded edges on a solid {colour} ground, a limited palette of {two or three accents}, flat geometric accents, soft long shadows, completely flat 2D; a clean rectangular banner just below the subject in bold geometric capitals.
- **Papercraft diorama.** Layered papercraft: {subject}, every element hand-cut from coloured construction paper with visible paper thickness and real shadows between layers, a {colour} paper ground, slightly uneven scissor cuts, soft studio light on the layers; a cut-paper banner just below the subject in chunky paper letters.
- **Pop-art comic.** A vintage comic panel: {subject}, bold black ink outlines, dot-screen shading, flat process colours ({palette}), starburst accents, a thick panel border, aged newsprint, slight ink misregistration; a caption box just below the subject in bold comic lettering.
- **Claymation.** A stop-motion clay scene: a handmade plasticine {subject} with visible fingerprints and clay texture, a {colour} clay backdrop and floor, chunky clay props, warm soft studio light like a miniature set, shallow depth of field; a small clay sign beside the subject in hand-moulded letters.

## Picture kinds: styles built from a client's own colours

Before writing, settle four slots from the client's style guide: **ground** (the neutral ground, light or dark), **ink** (the drawing and type colour), **accent** (one colour, used sparingly) and **type feel** ("clean modern grotesque sans"). Keep the accent scarce, one element per frame; scarcity makes it read as designed rather than generated.

- **Monoline editorial.** {Subject} drawn entirely in thin single-weight {ink} lines on a clean {ground}; sparse, generous empty space, a few small line details, one restrained {accent} element (a thin underline sweep or a small arc); a small caption just below the subject in {type feel}, {ink}, spaced capitals with a thin {accent} underline. Precise, refined, line only apart from the single accent.
- **Swiss typographic.** The label words set enormous in bold {type feel}, {ink} on {ground}, running across the frame above the subject with tight leading and cropped edges; a small black-and-white photographic cutout of {subject} on a thin baseline beneath them, on an asymmetric grid with one thin {accent} rule and a small {accent} square as the only colour; faint grid lines, precise margins. Flat, printed, matte. Good for title cards.
- **Wireglow.** {Subject} as a thin light-grey wireframe drawing on a near-black {ground} with fine film grain; from {the focal object} a soft narrow beam of {accent} light, the only colour; faint concentric guide circles; a caption just below the subject in {type feel}, light grey, spaced capitals, with a hairline rule beneath.
- **Duotone screenprint.** A dramatic photograph of {subject} reproduced as a two-colour screenprint, {ink} for the shadows and {accent} for the highlights, on off-white {ground} paper with coarse halftone grain and slight misregistration; a strong diagonal, the figure large and cropped; a wide solid {ink} bar just below the figure carrying the label reversed out in bold condensed {type feel}.

How each animates: monoline and Swiss as drawing motions (lines extend, the accent sweep draws itself, type settles slightly); wireglow as a beam pulse and a slow turn of the wireframe; duotone as grain shimmer. Any camera move goes in the plan's `camera_move`, never in the brief.

## For documentary-explainer, advertisement-product and social-ugc

- **When to use:** an idea made concrete ("the inbox is a junk drawer"), or where a distinct owned style beats stock footage.
- **Not for:** a demonstration, a testimonial, or anything that needs a real product or place as evidence; those start from footage or an approved picture of the real thing.

## For advertisement-product and social-ugc

- The idea reads in the first still with the sound off: front-load the concept in beat 1.
- Check whether the channel requires AI-generated media to be labelled. Never fabricate a statistic or a testimonial.

## For advertisement-product and training-education

- The client-colour styles above, with the slots taken from the client's style guide.
