The place: a chimney brick kiln on a dry plain in rural Tamil Nadu, morning, dry. The tall tapering brick chimney in the left third, rising to near the top of the frame, grey-white smoke drawing steadily from its top and drifting across the sky toward frame right; at its foot the long earth-banked firing trench running from the left third into the middle distance, smoke only, no flame; the rows of green bricks drying on the bare earth from the middle of the frame away from the camera, right of the trench; a waist-high stack of bricks at the near end of the rows, right of centre; the earth path with wheel ruts along the bottom of the frame; a flat plain with a few trees behind. Morning sun from frame left, moderately hard, neutral daylight; the smoke softens the sky behind the chimney; a faint heat shimmer over the trench; a little dust lifts from the path. The two workers are the only people.

The camera is locked off at standing eye level on the path; the frame does not move, zoom or drift. Wide: the chimney in the left third, the two workers small, right of centre, at the near end of the drying rows beside the stack, three-quarter toward the camera.

Both workers do everything with the right hand: the first lifts and passes with his right hand, the second receives and sets with his right hand, the left hand only steadying.

The steps, in this order, each one completed before the next begins:
1. At the start the first worker, in the orange vest, is bent at the waist with his right hand on a pair of dried green bricks lying in the nearest row; the second, in the purple shirt, stands upright at the stack beside him, his right hand free and reaching toward him.
2. The first worker lifts the pair of green bricks from the row with his right hand and straightens, holding them at waist height.
3. He hands the pair across to the second worker, about an arm's length, at waist height; the second takes them with his right hand.
4. The second worker sets the pair on top of the stack with his right hand and lets go; the stack is a little higher.
5. The first worker straightens with empty hands and the second rests his right hand on the stack. The shot ends there.

Throughout, the smoke keeps drawing from the chimney. One continuous shot, no cuts. No dialogue, no music, no captions or text.
