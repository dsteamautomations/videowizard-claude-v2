The place: a house site on red earth in rural Tamil Nadu, morning, dry. The low fresh plinth runs across the lower half of the frame from left to right at knee height, its grey cement top seen from slightly above, dusty with sand and grit. The neat stack of wire-cut bricks behind the plinth's right end, nobody at it. The coconut tree at the left beyond the plinth. The bucket with the mug stands on the red earth at the plinth's foot near the middle. Morning sun from the left of frame and behind the camera, moderately hard, neutral-warm daylight, short shadows falling to the right. A light breeze moves the coconut fronds; a little red dust rises from the broom and settles. Only one person in the frame.

The camera is locked off a little above eye level, looking slightly down; the frame does not move, zoom or drift. Wide: the worker full figure, the plinth across the lower half of the frame.

The worker does everything with his right hand. He stands on the red earth at the near side of the plinth, his left side toward the camera, three-quarter front, and never crosses to the far side.

The steps, in this order, each one completed before the next begins:
1. He starts at the plinth's left end, bent forward from the waist, the broom in his right hand with its tip on the plinth top, his left hand resting on his knee, head down.
2. He sweeps the plinth top with the broom in short strokes away from his body, stepping slowly to the right along the plinth's near side as he goes; sand and grit fall off the far edge and a little red dust rises and settles; the swept stretch of the top goes from dusty to clean.
3. When he reaches the middle of the plinth, beside the bucket, he stops sweeping, bends, and lays the broom down with his right hand on the red earth at the plinth's foot.
4. With his right hand he lifts the mug out of the bucket; the bucket stays on the earth at his feet.
5. With his right hand he throws the mugful of water along the swept plinth top in a flat arc; the top darkens where the water lands.
6. He dips the mug into the bucket with his right hand and throws a second mugful the same way; the swept stretch is now dark and wet.
7. He stands a little bent near the middle of the plinth, the mug in his right hand at the end of the throw, the bucket at his feet, the broom on the earth, head down watching the water settle. The shot ends there.
