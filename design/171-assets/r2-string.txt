The place: a house site on red earth in rural Tamil Nadu, morning, dry. The plinth's grey top, still dark in damp patches from wetting, fills the middle band of the frame; its top edge runs from the near-right corner away to the upper left. The wooden peg stands driven into the red earth just beyond the corner at the lower right. The thin white cotton line is tied to a far peg at the plinth's far end, up the frame to the left, and lies slack on the earth along the plinth's edge toward the near peg. The brick stack soft in the background at the top right. Morning sun from the left of frame, moderately hard, neutral-warm daylight; the shadows of coconut fronds move a little on the earth. Nobody else in the frame; the worker's body stays out of frame.

The camera is locked off low, about knee height, looking along the plinth's edge so the line reads as one straight stroke; the frame does not move, zoom or drift. Medium shot: the peg and the hands at the lower right, the line running diagonally away to the upper left.

The steps, in this order, each one completed before the next begins:
1. At the start the left hand steadies the head of the peg and the right hand holds the free end of the line beside the peg; the line lies slack on the earth all the way to the far peg.
2. The right hand draws the line back toward the near peg; the slack lifts off the earth and the line straightens until it runs taut and dead straight along the plinth's top edge from the far peg to the hand.
3. The right hand winds the taut line twice round the top of the near peg and tucks the end under itself.
4. The right hand plucks the taut line once; it quivers and settles straight.
5. The right hand lifts off the line; the left hand stays on the peg; the line stays taut and straight along the edge. The shot ends there.
