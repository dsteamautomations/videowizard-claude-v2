## 2. New fixes

**Every fix below is a rule for any film**: a documentary, a product or service explainer, a clinic or hospital film, a food, factory or software film, a testimonial. The "Why" lines quote VW-6 only as evidence; the rules name no subject. In short, what each one means anywhere:

| Fix | The rule, for any film |
|---|---|
| N1, N2, N3 | Between the first and last frame of a moving shot, every change has a visible cause and fits the seconds; the clip is checked for things that appear, vanish, morph or repeat. |
| N4, N5 | Scenes have the life the narration implies, and every movement has a visible goal. |
| N6, N7 | Anything real that is shown (a material, a process, a place, a tool, a procedure) follows sourced facts and normal practice, at a believable scale and in a believable place. |
| N8, N17 | The picture shows the exact step or claim being spoken, with evidence for claims, and never its opposite. |
| N9, N15, N16 | Every shot belongs to one world (light, colour, look), fills the frame, and joins its neighbours with a chosen transition. |
| N10 | The film's own subject (the client's product, technique, service or place) is shown as itself; any alternative appears only as a labelled contrast the operator approved. |
| N11, N12, N13 | No message twice, every planned step of a build actually appears, and every label reads plainly at phone size. |
| N14 | Steps of one process share one place, crew, light and the state carried over from the step before. |

### N1. Every start and end pair comes with a change list

**Why.** A pair is approved as two good stills. Nothing lists what differs between them or who causes each difference, so the video model invents a way to get from one to the other. On VW-6 that meant bricks, open shutters and a growing heap of concrete, all appearing by themselves.

- With every pair, the planner writes a change list: each visible difference between the start and the end, the subject who causes it, the action that causes it, and when it happens within the shot.
- Gemini compares the two images and lists the differences it sees. Any difference that has no cause in the change list is shown, and the pair is refused until it has one or the end image is changed.
- The page shows the change list and the motion text beside the pair, so the reviewer does not have to imagine the clip.
- Where: `visuals_stage.prepare_take`, `image_check` (a pair check), `templates/visuals.html`.

### N2. An action budget

**Why.** Three bricks in 8 s, or a full roof pour from one pan, cannot be shown honestly in the time given.

- Each action in the change list carries a realistic minimum duration, from the planner's process facts (N6), for example "lay and tap one brick: about 3 s", "pour and stir one cup of tea: about 4 s", "draw blood into one tube: about 6 s".
- The check refuses a shot whose actions cannot fit its seconds. The planner then drops actions or splits the shot.
- Where: plan schema (`actions[].min_s`), `validate_plan`.

### N3. Every take is checked before the operator watches it

**Why.** Gemini checks pictures before the operator sees them, but it does not check video clips.

- Each retained take is sent to Gemini (which reads video) with fixed questions:
  - Does anything appear or vanish without a visible cause?
  - Do the tools, clothes and faces stay the same?
  - Is any action repeated without its precondition (pouring from an empty pan)?
  - Does any object change shape or size?
  - Does the clip end in the planned end state?
- The answers are shown on the take's card with the times. A failed `must` marks the take, and the operator decides whether to keep it or make another, which is paid and announced.
- Where: `image_check` (a video variant), `visual_workers` on retain.

### N4. Life in the frame

**Why.** The planner is told not to add anything unrequested, so a "busy construction site" comes out empty unless the plan names the activity.

- The treatment states each scene's activity level from the narration and the brief: quiet, normal or busy.
- For normal and busy scenes, the plan lists the background activity: who else is there, what they do, and the working equipment. These are named secondary subjects with their own beats, held to the same continuity rules.
- The check warns when a work scene has a single subject and no background activity.
- Where: prompt, plan schema (scene `activity_level`, `background_activity`), `validate_plan`.

### N5. Visible intent

**Why.** A person walking or reaching without a visible goal reads as aimless.

- Every moving subject's beat names its target, and the target is in frame at the start or comes into view during the shot: the doorway, the counter, the machine, the patient, the wall being built.
- The check refuses a movement beat without a target.
- Where: plan schema (beat `target` required for movement), `validate_plan`, prompt.

### N6. Facts for scenes, processes and materials

**Why.** design/89 D gives technical drawings a sourced spec, but scenes have no equivalent. How clay is dug, what green bricks look like, mortar against concrete, what a testing lab contains: all of this was left to the image model's guess.

- For every process, material and setting a scene depicts, the planner writes short **visual facts**. Each fact names what it looks like, how it is done, the usual setting and the tools, plus its source and status, as in 89 D2 and D3.
- Sources, in order: the client's material; the footage (the analysis already describes materials, tools and settings clip by clip); a web source under the web rule; an approved reference image.
- **Materials and processes join the reference round** beside people, objects and places. A material, process or setting the film relies on (an unburnt brick, a lab instrument, a dish at a given stage of cooking, a hospital ward, a factory line) gets its own frame or picture, approved once.
- An unknown fact is never invented. The shot avoids the close detail, or the operator is asked.
- Where: plan schema (`visual_facts`), the reference round (element kinds `material`, `process`, `setting`), packet, prompt.

### N7. Setting and practice are plausible

**Why.** A room under construction in the middle of a paddy field, a room too small to use, and a pan of mortar set down on fresh mortar are each things a site engineer would notice at once.

- The visual facts (N6) cover where the work usually happens and how people usually do it.
- Every picture's check gains two fixed lines:
  - "The setting and its scale are plausible for what is shown (location, size of spaces, ground)."
  - "People handle tools and materials as a practitioner would."
- The keyframe's human-scale anchor (design/77 L39) extends to spaces: a room shows its usable size against a person.
- Where: `image_check` (fixed lines), prompt.

### N8. A picture never contradicts the words

**Why.** design/89 C1 covers claims for graphics only. "Well burnt" over wet-looking bricks is the same failure in a scene.

- C1's claim lines apply to every shot. A picture may leave a claim out, but it must never show its opposite.
- The checklist gains one `must` line per descriptive word the narration uses about the subject (well burnt, uniform, smooth, cooler…).
- Where: prompt, answer form, `image_check`.

### N9. One world

**Why.** Three bricks on a neutral studio background do not belong in a documentary shot on location.

- AI stills and takes sit in the film's world: its places, light and look, matched to the footage (89 and the library's gap-fill guide).
- A studio or neutral background is allowed only when the treatment says so, with the reason.
- The check warns on a plain background in a film whose treatment does not allow one.
- **Light and colour match the neighbours, measured.** For every AI shot next to footage, the server compares average brightness, colour temperature and saturation with the neighbouring clips' frames. It warns on a large jump. The motion or image prompt then states the neighbours' light ("soft overcast light, muted greens"), from the footage character (design/88).
- Where: prompt, `image_check` (a fixed line), treatment.

### N10. The subject itself

**Why.** Graphics in a rat-trap film drew conventional bond twice, and nobody asked the operator.

- Any picture or graphic that depicts the film's subject (the client's product, technique or service) shows the subject's own form.
- An alternative (the conventional way, a competitor's way) appears only as a contrast the narration calls for, labelled as such.
- The treatment lists, per graphic, what it depicts ("rat-trap wall in elevation", "conventional wall, as the contrast"; "our app's checkout screen", "a generic competitor screen, as the contrast"), so the operator approves it before anything is drawn. The drawing spec (89 D4) repeats it.
- Where: treatment, prompt, `validate_plan` (a graphic naming the subject with no depiction line is refused).

### N11. No repeated message

**Why.** A truck still, then a diagram that says the same thing; two "fewer bricks" graphics three minutes apart, the second adding nothing.

- Each shot's purpose names the message it carries.
- Two shots that carry the same message are allowed only when the second adds something new (a number, a cause, a consequence), and the plan says what.
- The check compares shot purposes and claims across the film and warns on a repeat. It also warns when two **consecutive** shots show the same subject with the same message (an object, then the same object again; a vehicle, then an icon of the same vehicle; a dish, then the same dish plated).
- The preferred fix is to **merge the pair into one shot in context**, moving where possible, and let the next shot add something new. For example, a truck being loaded at the kiln yard instead of a still of a parked truck, followed by a graphic that adds the figures; or bricks being checked by hand in a stack at the site instead of three bricks on a plain background, followed by the row.
- **An isolated object needs a reason.** An object shown alone, outside its place of use (a "product shot"), is allowed only when the treatment asks for that look (N9). Otherwise it is shown where it is made, kept or used, in a person's hands or on the site.
- Where: plan schema (shot `message`), `validate_plan`, prompt.

### N12. A build that actually builds

**Why.** A graphic covering single-, two- and multi-storey buildings showed only the single storey for 23.7 s. The film alone does not show whether the later states were never planned, never generated, or dropped at assembly.

- A graphic that covers N claims is planned with at least N states (89 C1, B1).
- Before recording, the server checks that each state exists, is approved, and differs visibly from the previous one (a fingerprint check, as in 89 H6).
- After building the film, assembly checks that each state appears in the output for its planned frames.
- Where: `validate_plan`, `visuals_stage.sequence`, `stage3/assembly.py`.

### N13. Labels say what they mean

- Every label is a complete phrase ("25% fewer bricks", not "↓ 25% bricks"; "2 hours faster", not "↑2h").
- Every leader line ends on the part it names.
- Every number shown has its unit and its source (89 D).
- The checklist gains one `must` line per label and per leader line.
- **Text is large enough for a phone:** labels at least about 3.5% of the frame height, titles larger. The code-drawn route (89 E) enforces it, and the image check measures it for AI graphics.
- Where: prompt, the library's technical-drawing guide (89 D6), `image_check`.

### N14. Consecutive shots agree on the state of the world

**Why.** At 3:10–3:14 the film shows a finished plinth being moistened, and the narration then says the bricks are laid on the plinth. The next shots (3:20–3:32) are real footage of bricks laid straight on bare sandy ground, at what looks like a demonstration site. No plinth appears in them. Each shot is fine alone. Together they tell a process that does not happen, because nothing checks that shots presented as steps of one process show the same world.

- When the narration presents shots as steps of one process ("then", "on top of", "next"), the planner writes the **state that carries over** between them: what was built in the previous step and must still be visible ("the plinth under the first course", "the bandage already on the arm", "the dough already risen").
- The first cut and visuals both check it against what each clip shows. The analysis already lists each clip's landmarks and props, so a clip that lacks the carried-over state is flagged.
- A flagged step is filled another way:
  - a different clip that shows the state
  - a reframed part of the same clip where it is visible
  - an AI shot that shows the step on the right base
  - or the narration's wording is put to the operator as a mismatch to accept
- **One place per process.** Steps of one process happen in one place, at one time of day, with one crew, unless the narration moves somewhere else. On VW-6 a single process cuts between at least three settings: a green-tank yard, a plinth site and an AI room in a paddy field.
  - The planner names the process's **setting** (a place element, with its approved reference, N6) and its light and time of day.
  - Every step uses that setting:
    - **real clips:** clips of the same location. The analysis already groups clips by landmarks and by `color_match_group_id`, so clips from one site can be found together.
    - **AI shots:** the setting's reference and its landmarks, with the same light.
  - The same people (the crew) carry over as identity references.
  - A change of place mid-process is allowed only when the narration moves, and the plan says so.
  - The check warns when consecutive steps of one process come from different location groups or show different landmarks.
- Where: `stage2` (options check, from the clip logs' landmarks and colour groups), `validate_plan` (carried-over state and setting between consecutive shots), prompt.

### N17. The picture shows the step being narrated, not a nearby one

**Why.** When the narration names a specific step (mixing mortar and fixing guide strings, pointing, curing) or makes a claim that needs evidence (strength comparable to conventional bonds), the film shows loosely related footage instead: bricks being laid, a repeated wall, window shutters. The first cut matches clips to passages broadly. A clip that shows the right activity in general but the wrong step passes. Nothing turns "no clip shows this step" into a gap for visuals to fill.

- The first cut matches clips to each **step or claim**, not only to the passage. The planner splits the narration into steps (one action or one claim each, using 89 A1's timed sentences).
- For each step, a clip qualifies only when its analysis shows that step: the action, the tool, the material. If no clip does, the step becomes a gap. Visuals then fills it with an AI shot of that exact step, or with a graphic for a claim that needs evidence (a load test, a table of strengths).
- A claim that needs evidence ("strong", "comparable", "cooler") is never illustrated by an unrelated pretty picture. It gets the evidence, or a scene that clearly shows the result (a three-storey building in the technique; a patient walking after the procedure; the product working under load), and the plan says which.
- The check lists every step whose shot does not show its key action. It reads the analysis for footage and the plan for AI shots.
- Where: `stage2` (options prompt and validator: step-level matching, gaps for unmatched steps), `validate_plan`, prompt.

### N15. Every shot fills the frame

**Why.** The drone clip is wider than 16:9, and assembly pads it with black bars instead of filling the frame.

- A source whose aspect differs from the film's is **cropped to fill**. The planner chooses the crop (which part of the picture to keep) and states it.
- Bars only appear when the treatment asks for them, with a reason (a deliberate cinematic frame).
- Assembly measures each shot's black edges and reports any.
- Where: `stage3/assembly.py`, stage 2 render, plan schema (`crop` per footage fill).

### N16. Transitions are chosen

**Why.** Every cut in the film is a hard cut, including changes of medium where a short dissolve, or a match on shape or movement, would carry the eye.

- At every cut the planner chooses a transition: a hard cut (the default inside one scene); a short dissolve of 6–12 frames (a change of medium, place or time); or a match cut (the same shape or movement across the cut, such as a real wall into its diagram). It states the reason.
- The check warns on a change of medium with no chosen transition.
- Where: plan schema (`transition_in` per shot), assembly, prompt.

## 3. Answers to the questions raised

- **R4, mortar against concrete.** Mortar is cement, sand and water, and has no stones. Concrete adds coarse aggregate, which is grey crushed stone, not black pebbles. If the shot means mortar, the picture is wrong. If it means pouring a roof slab, aggregate is right but its colour is wrong. The plan never said which material it was, so the image model guessed. N6 makes the material a sourced fact with its own reference.
- **R5, the pit.** Who decides today: the planner writes a sentence about the scene, and GPT Image draws its own idea of it. No one checks how the work is really done. In practice, clay for bricks is often dug from shallow borrow pits beside the kiln, so a pit is not wrong in itself. Standing knee-deep in a wet pit, on the other hand, is the image model's invention. N6 puts this to the footage first, or to a reference, before the scene is drawn.
- **R13, the plinth.** In Indian practice the plinth is the base raised above the ground, commonly 450–600 mm, built in concrete or plastered masonry. So a plinth visible above the ground is normal, and a pan of water fits a surface being moistened. It does not go underground. The problems are where the shot sits: it plays over different words (89 A), it appears twice (89 H), and the next shots of brick-laying are footage from a different place with no plinth at all (R18, N14). These are general building facts I am confident of; the client's own drawings, if any, decide.

## Not covered

- server/tests (Docker).
- How reliably Gemini answers the take questions (N3) and the pair differences (N1). It marks; the operator decides.
- The action durations (N2) are estimates. They come from the visual facts, and the operator can override them.
- The transcription used for the line-by-line table is automatic. A few words may differ from the script, but none of the findings rests on a single word.

**Batch 1 as built (N1, N2, N3):**

- **N1.**
  - Each video shot carries `changes`: what differs between its start and end picture, and the beat (`beat_id`) of the subject that causes it. When it happens is that beat's frames.
  - A change naming a beat that is not in the shot is refused. A video shot with no change list warns this release and is refused from the next.
  - `prepare-take` copies the changes into the take request. When the shot has a change list, it asks Gemini Flash once for the visible differences between the start and the end picture and matches them to the changes. A difference no change explains is marked on the preparation, on the Takes step and in the reply, and the operator approves the pair or changes the end image. One check per pair: the same two pictures and the same list are not checked twice. A Gemini error is recorded and never blocks.
- **N2.**
  - Each beat carries `min_s`, the realistic minimum time for its action, from the facts of the process.
  - A beat shorter than its `min_s` is refused. So is a subject whose beats need more seconds than the shot has.
  - A video shot whose acting beats have no `min_s` warns.
- **N3.**
  - When a take is ready, the take worker sends it to Gemini Flash with five fixed questions:
    1. Does anything appear or vanish without a visible cause?
    2. Do the tools, clothes and faces stay the same?
    3. Is any action repeated without its precondition?
    4. Does any object change shape or size?
    5. Does the clip end where the plan says it ends?
  - The shot's change list and motion text go with the questions as the plan.
  - Each answer gives pass or fail, what was seen and the second it happens. The answers go on the take's card and in `vw visuals status`.
  - A failed must line marks the take. The operator keeps it or asks for another; nothing is taken again automatically. A Gemini error is recorded on the take and never blocks.
