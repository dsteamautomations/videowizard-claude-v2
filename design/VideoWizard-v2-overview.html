Who does what

OperatorPicks the client, drops in the script and the clips, answers a few questions, and says yes or no at each stage. Never sees code or files on the server.
AssistantThe chat. Reads the stage instructions, does the creative writing and proposing, shows results, asks the one approval question, and runs the tool.
ServerOwns the order of stages. Checks every proposal against the rules, renders the films, records each stage's output and the operator's words, keeps costs.
Paid servicesElevenLabs for the voice, Gemini 3.8 Flash for watching the clips, OpenAI for generated images and video. The assistant tells you before a paid step; it never asks permission.

The twelve stages, and what each one leaves behind

1 · intakewords

The run starts with a client chosen from the company directory and the script document. The assistant separates the spoken lines from everything else in the document and suggests up to three titles. You pick one, or type your own.

Creates the clean script, the original document, and the title on the run.
2 · briefwordsChanged 15 Oct

Five decisions about the film: purpose, audience, message, tone, ending. The assistant proposes them from the script and asks only the questions that would change the film. Your answers are stored in your own words. Production details come here too: target length, process stages, brand rules, languages.

Brand and safety rules are asked for explicitly. None are filled in from examples, so a rule such as "everyone wears PPE" applies only when you give it.

Creates the brief with its reasons and your answers.
3 · scriptwordsChanged 15 Oct

One wording pass against the brief. Every proposed change is shown as before and after with a reason. You accept all, some, or keep the original. The accepted text is split into numbered beats: a beat is one paragraph of the script (the earlier application called it a passage, P001, P002; BEAT_01 is P001).

A paragraph beat is only the starting point. In options you can split the narration into scenes at sentence starts instead, without changing the script or re-recording.

Creates the final spoken text with beats BEAT_01, BEAT_02 and so on. Everything after this is bound to this exact text.
4 · voicepaidChanged 15 Oct

Choosing a voice: catalogue browsing, previews, up to six short auditions on one passage, then the full narration. You listen to the files the assistant downloads for you. ElevenLabs is paid; the assistant tells you, then proceeds.

Creates narration.mp3 and a timing file with the start and end of every word. The narration is never re-recorded and its words never change. It is cut only between sentences, at pauses you approve in options, and silence is added there.
5 · mediapictures inChanged 15 Oct

You copy the clips into the run folder. The tool lists them with length, size and sound, skips exact duplicates, converts phone clips with variable frame rate, and uploads each one with a receipt.

Three clips upload at a time. A dropped connection is retried after 5, 15 and 30 seconds, and large files are checked by size instead of being downloaded again.

Creates the clip list that every later stage refers to.
6 · analysispaidChanged 15 Oct

Each clip is watched once by Gemini 3.8 Flash, which writes a detailed log: usable sub-clips with exact frames, who is talking and when, camera and movement, clean sound intervals, safety flags. The server then works out, for every sub-clip, whether it can be used as is, cropped to hide a speaker, or not at all. Five clips run at a time.

A client rule can be dropped afterwards, for example "no PPE rule for this client". Every clip is then re-checked from the saved logs at no cost, without watching the clips again.

Creates one log per clip and a 10-line summary card. Ask the assistant for a clip's card at any time.
7 · optionsdeciding the cutChanged 15–16 Oct

Scenes and pauses. When the script is one paragraph, the assistant proposes scenes, each starting at a sentence and tied to a process stage from the brief. It also proposes pauses, only after a sentence ends, at most 4 seconds each, with a reason. The recording's own gap counts toward each pause. One yes from you approves both.

Exact matches first. Real footage goes only where it shows exactly what is being said, cut into short shots of about 2 to 3.5 seconds. Everything else is a blank, at least 3 seconds, for AI video or animation later. Each clip is used once.

Decisions. B-roll, a second use of any clip, a clip to check by eye, and a poor-quality match each come behind a decision slide.

The assistant first shows a coverage report, then proposes a first and, where possible, a backup option for each scene. Before rendering, it shows the seconds of real footage, blanks and decisions in each scene. The server refuses anything that breaks the editing rules, then renders the review film.

Numbers on screen. Each slide shows its number large, in amber. Every real shot carries its number in the top-left corner, for example #08, or #11 DECISION for a placement you decide on.

Limits. You can ask for changes to the review film as often as you like; each new review film takes about 10 minutes to render. From the fourth round, the assistant first suggests leaving the scenes still not approved blank, and asks which scenes to keep trying. If the server refuses the assistant's plan three times in a row, it stops and asks you to retry, revise an earlier stage, file a defect, or stop.

Creates the review film with numbered slides: #05 a scene, BACKUP for #05, DECISION with its question, and BLANK cards that the narration plays under. It is a lighter 720p copy, so it uploads quickly. You watch the film.
8 · masterdeciding the cutChanged 15–16 Oct

You answer every numbered slide by its number, for example "#05 keep, #06 reject, #12 drop". Scene and backup slides: keep, reject with a reason, or keep with a small trim. Decision slides: keep or drop. Blank slides need no answer. A dropped placement becomes part of a blank.

The server checks that nothing is used twice unless you kept a reuse, applies the trims within the rules, and builds a fresh master. Your rejection reasons are remembered for this client's future films.

Limits. You can change your answers and rebuild the master as often as you like. If recording the master fails, for example because a file is missing, it is tried three times before you are asked how to proceed.

Creates the master film with BLANK cards wherever pictures are still to come, and one gap per blank, each with its intent and the words under it.
9 · first_cutdeciding the cut

One question: did you watch the master film and listen to it, and do you approve it as the first cut? Your reply must say both.

Creates the approved first cut, locked to the exact film file.
10 · visualspaidChanged 15 Oct

For each gap, the assistant plans what the picture should be, gathers reference images (a frame from a clip, a photo you supply, or a generated image from OpenAI), and either generates a short video take or uses a still or movie you provide. You approve each reference by looking at it, and each take by watching it.

Gaps now include the blanks inside scenes, each with what it must show. One generated shot is at most 30 seconds, so a longer blank is made as several shots.

Creates the chosen take or asset for every gap, with its references.
11 · assemblyfinishingChanged 15 Oct

The master plus the gap fills becomes the whole film. You choose per fill how a take sits in its slot, and for any shaky shot whether to leave it, stabilise it, or swap it. The clips' own clean sound is placed quietly under the narration, no music. You watch and listen, ask for changes, or accept.

The narration is laid with the pauses you approved; its words are untouched.

Creates the complete film as one MP4, with loudness measured and recorded.
12 · deliverymacChanged 15 Oct

The package is written: the approved film, the narration, the original clips used, the normalised fills, the plans, subtitles, a finishing checklist for the editor, and a media-pool sheet. Then the one Mac step: pull the run on the Mac and run the Resolve step once. It builds a DaVinci Resolve 21.1.1 timeline that reproduces the film's cuts and crops, exports it, reopens it to verify, and uploads it. Back here, you confirm five things: watched, listened, read the subtitles, timeline verified, package complete. Then the run closes and keeps only what matters.

Subtitles have one line per sentence, timed with the pauses. In Resolve the narration is placed as pieces on whole frames, one per stretch between pauses, and the check after reopening covers those pieces too.

Creates the delivery package with Approved-film.mp4 and Approved-timeline.drt, ready for the editor.

Five rules that hold everywhere

  • The server owns the order. The assistant asks "what is next" and does that. It cannot skip, repeat, or go back on its own.
  • Your yes is the record. A stage is accepted only by your explicit reply to the one question. The reply is stored word for word. "ok" or "looks fine" is asked again.
  • Paid steps inform, never ask. The assistant says which provider and proceeds. Costs are recorded per step.
  • Three strikes. A proposal refused three times stops and asks you how to proceed: retry, revise an earlier stage, file a defect, or stop.
  • Anything wrong is a defect, not a debugging session. The assistant files it and stops. The builder reads the server record and fixes the cause.

Where things are

On this machine, everything for one run lives in runs/VW-n/: in/ for what you drop in, out/ for what the assistant and the tool produce, work/ for scratch that is deleted at close. The server keeps every stage's output, your approvals, costs and the files, and the web page at /runs lists every run with its stage.
