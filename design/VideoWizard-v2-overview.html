Who does what

OperatorPicks the client from the company directory, drops in the script and the clips, answers a few questions, and says yes or no at each stage: in chat, or on the voices page and the review page. Never sees code or files on the server.
AssistantThe chat. Reads the stage instructions, does the creative writing and proposing, shows results, asks the one approval question, and runs the tool.
ServerOwns the order of stages. Checks every proposal against the rules, renders the films, records each stage's output and the operator's words, keeps costs. The two pages and the chat call the same server steps, so a click on a page and a yes in chat are the same record.
Paid servicesElevenLabs for the voice, Gemini 3.8 Flash for watching the clips, OpenAI for generated images and video. The assistant tells you before a paid step; it never asks permission.

The twelve stages, and what each one leaves behind

1 · intakewordsChanged 20 Oct

The run starts with a client chosen from the company directory and the script document. The directory's details about the client, its website, audience, call to action and brand files, travel with the run from here: the brief, the script and the delivery all use them, and the assistant warns you wherever your words or the script say something different.

The assistant separates the spoken lines from everything else in the document and suggests up to three titles. Before you approve, it shows you the kept narration exactly as it will be recorded, so a dropped heading or a missed sentence is caught here, not at the voice.

Creates the clean script, the original document, the client card and the title on the run.
2 · briefwordsChanged 20–21 Oct

Five decisions about the film: purpose, audience, message, tone, ending. The assistant proposes them from the script and asks only the questions that would change the film. Your answers are stored in your own words. Production details come here too: the languages in the footage and of the narration, whether the speaker is on camera, brand and safety rules, and the film's format: the frame shape, where it plays, and whether it is watched muted.

Chapters. The brief names the chapters of the film: plain names for what the footage will show, such as "Mixing the mortar" or "Pointing the joints", in film order. They are proposed from this script and never copied from the client's last film. Later, every clip is tagged with a chapter, and that is how footage finds its scene. A heading-style name such as "Chapter 3" is refused.

Brand and safety. If you say there are no brand or safety rules, the usual PPE flags (gloves, hard hats, footwear) are waived from the start of the analysis, and you are not asked again later. Any rule you do give is kept.

Creates the brief with its reasons, the chapters, the format and your answers.
3 · scriptwords

One wording pass against the brief. Every proposed change is shown as before and after with a reason. You accept all, some, or keep the original. The accepted text is split into numbered beats: a beat is one paragraph of the script. A paragraph beat is only the starting point; in options the narration is cut into scenes at sentence starts, without changing the script or re-recording.

Creates the final spoken text with beats BEAT_01, BEAT_02 and so on. Everything after this is bound to this exact text.
4 · voicepaidChanged 20 Oct

The direction first. Before any voice is heard, the assistant proposes how the narration should be read: an overall register, such as measured, and a few sentences marked for a particular delivery. You approve it, and the same direction travels with every tryout and every full narration, so what you hear is what the film will carry.

The voices page. Choosing the voice happens on a page, not in chat. Browse the live ElevenLabs catalogue with filters for language, accent, gender, age and use, play samples, and shortlist. Try up to six voices on a short passage of your own script. Make up to three full narrations in different voices. Click "Make this the final narration", with your words, and that click is the record of the stage. Favourites are kept for the next film, and a library voice is saved to the account for you when you first try it.

ElevenLabs is paid; the page and the assistant say so, then proceed.

Creates narration.mp3 and a timing file with the start and end of every word. The narration is never re-recorded and its words never change. It is cut only between sentences, at pauses you approve in options.
5 · mediapictures inChanged 20 Oct

You copy the clips into the run folder. The tool lists them one line per clip, with length, size and sound, and the assistant shows you that list to confirm. It skips exact duplicates and converts clips with a variable frame rate. One question: may the camera sound be used in the film? Then the upload, three clips at a time, each with a receipt; a dropped connection is retried.

On a film of 46 clips and about 900 MB the upload took three and a half minutes.

Creates the clip list that every later stage refers to.
6 · analysispaidChanged 20 Oct

Each clip is watched once by Gemini 3.8 Flash, which writes a detailed log: usable sub-clips with exact frames, who is talking and when, camera and movement, clean sound intervals, safety flags, and the chapter each sub-clip belongs to. The server then works out, for every sub-clip, whether it can be used as is, cropped to hide a speaker, or not at all.

Fifteen clips run at a time. A typical clip takes about four and a half minutes of model time; 46 clips were done in about 18 minutes. The assistant reports the counts while it waits. A clip the model fails on is retried on your word, one more paid call.

A safety rule can still be waived afterwards, and every clip is re-checked from the saved logs at no cost.

Creates one log per clip and a 10-line summary card. Ask the assistant for a clip's card at any time.
7 · optionsdeciding the cutChanged 21 Oct

Scenes. On every film the assistant proposes the scenes: one per paragraph, each starting at a sentence, with a title and the chapter it covers, plus any pauses, only after a sentence ends, at most 4 seconds each, with a reason. One yes from you approves them. From then on every scene has an exact start and end in the recorded narration.

Exact matches first. Real footage goes only where it shows exactly what is being said, cut into short shots. Everything else is a blank, at least 3 seconds, for AI video or animation later. Each clip is used once. B-roll, a second use of any clip, a clip to check by eye, and a poor-quality match each come behind a decision slide.

The assistant first shows a coverage report, then writes a cut for each scene and, where possible, a backup cut. The server refuses anything that breaks the editing rules, then renders the review film: a numbered slide before each cut, the narration over the chosen footage, a captioned card over each blank, and a slide before each decision. A seven-minute review film renders in about three minutes.

The review page. You watch the review film on the review page, where the slides are listed beside it; clicking a slide moves the film to it. At the foot of the page you approve the options with your own words. That approval is the record of the stage.

Limits. You can ask for changes to the review film as often as you like. From the fourth round, the assistant first suggests leaving the scenes still not approved blank. If the server refuses the assistant's plan three times in a row, it stops and asks you how to proceed.

Creates the review film with numbered slides: #5 a scene's cut, #6 BACKUP for #5, #5A DECISION with its question, and BLANK cards that the narration plays under.
8 · masterdeciding the cutChanged 21 Oct

Answers by button, on the review page. After the options are approved, the same page turns on the buttons. For each scene's cut and its backup: Keep, Reject or Keep with edit. For each decision slide: Keep or Drop. Blank slides need nothing. You watch, click, and the answer is recorded at once with your name and the time; click another button to change it. Nothing is typed by number.

Two guards. A scene needs one cut, not two: if both the cut and its backup are kept, the page says so at once, under both rows. The decisions inside a cut you rejected do not need an answer; they fade out and leave the count.

When the last slide is answered, the assistant builds the master. The server checks that nothing is used twice unless you kept a reuse, applies the trims, swaps to the scene's other cut where a clip collides, and renders the master film, with a BLANK card wherever pictures are still to come.

If you prefer chat, the assistant can ask the slides there instead, one slide per question with the same buttons, never a list of labels to type.

Creates the master film and one gap per blank, each with its intent and the words under it.
9 · first_cutdeciding the cutMerged 20 Oct

This is no longer a separate step for you. One question after the master film: did you watch it and listen to it, and do you approve it as the first cut? Your reply must say both. That one reply records the master and the first cut together.

Creates the approved first cut, locked to the exact film file.
10 · visualspaidBeing revised

For each gap, the assistant plans what the picture should be, gathers reference images (a frame from a clip, a photo you supply, or a generated image), and either generates a short video take or uses a still or an animation your editor provides. You approve the treatment, each reference by looking at it, and each take by watching it. One generated shot is at most 30 seconds, so a longer blank is made as several shots.

The stage is being revised after the first full-size run, so this card is short. The treatment will be shown to you gap by gap and approved before any plan is written.

Creates the chosen take or asset for every gap, with its references.
11 · assemblyfinishing

The master plus the gap fills becomes the whole film. You choose per fill how a take sits in its slot, and for any shaky shot whether to leave it, stabilise it, or swap it. The clips' own clean sound is placed quietly under the narration, no music. The narration is laid with the pauses you approved; its words are untouched. You watch and listen, ask for changes, or accept.

Creates the complete film as one MP4, with loudness measured and recorded.
12 · deliverymacChanged 20 Oct

The package is written: the approved film, the narration, the original clips used, the normalised fills, the plans, subtitles with one line per sentence, a finishing checklist for the editor, and a media-pool sheet. The client's brand files from the directory go in a Graphics folder, and the checklist carries the brand lines, so the editor adds the logo and the end card in Resolve, where they stay editable. Then the one Mac step: pull the run on the Mac and run the Resolve step once. It builds a DaVinci Resolve timeline that reproduces the film's cuts and crops, exports it, reopens it to verify, and uploads it. Back here, you confirm five things: watched, listened, read the subtitles, timeline verified, package complete. Then the run closes and keeps only what matters.

Creates the delivery package with Approved-film.mp4 and Approved-timeline.drt, ready for the editor.

Seven rules that hold everywhere

  • The server owns the order. The assistant asks "what is next" and does that. It cannot skip, repeat, or go back on its own.
  • Your yes is the record. A stage is accepted only by your explicit reply to the one question, or by your click with your words on a page. The reply is stored word for word. "ok" or "looks fine" is asked again.
  • One flow, two front ends. Whatever a page does for you, the chat can do too, through the same server step, and the other side sees it at once. A page is never the only place a step exists.
  • Paid steps inform, never ask. The assistant says which provider and proceeds. Costs are recorded per step.
  • Three strikes. A proposal refused three times stops and asks you how to proceed: retry, revise an earlier stage, file a defect, or stop.
  • A lost connection is not a defect. If the server cannot be reached, the tool retries every 15 seconds for three minutes, then asks you to check the network and try once more. Only a second failure in a row becomes a defect.
  • Anything wrong is a defect, not a debugging session. The assistant files it and stops. While a defect is open the run accepts no new record. The builder reads the server record, fixes the cause and marks it fixed; then you resume with "what is next".

Where things are

On your machine, everything for one run lives in runs/VW-n/: in/ for what you drop in, out/ for what the assistant and the tool produce, work/ for scratch that is deleted at close. The server keeps every stage's output, your approvals, costs and the films. Its pages, all behind your sign-in:

  • /runsevery run with its stage
  • /runs/VW-none run: each stage's output, your words, the costs
  • /runs/VW-n/voices2the voices page: browse, try, narrate, final
  • /runs/VW-n/reviewthe review page: the film, the slides, your answers
  • /voicesthe voices directory: favourites and which voice narrated which film
  • /defectsthe defects filed from the chat and their fixes
