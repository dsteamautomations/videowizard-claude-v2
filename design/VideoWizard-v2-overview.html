VideoWizard v2, from script to delivered film

You talk to an assistant in a chat. It runs one tool, the server keeps the order and the record, and the film is built on this machine. One trip to the Mac, at the very end, for DaVinci Resolve.

Who does what

OperatorPicks the client, drops in the script and the clips, answers a few questions, and says yes or no at each stage. Never sees code or files on the server.
AssistantThe chat. Reads the stage instructions, does the creative writing and proposing, shows results, asks the one approval question, and runs the tool.
ServerOwns the order of stages. Checks every proposal against the rules, renders the films, records each stage's output and the operator's words, keeps costs.
Paid servicesElevenLabs for the voice, Gemini 3.8 Flash for watching the clips, OpenAI for generated images and video. The assistant tells you before a paid step; it never asks permission.

The twelve stages, and what each one leaves behind

1 · intakewords

The run starts with a client chosen from the company directory and the script document. The assistant separates the spoken lines from everything else in the document and suggests up to three titles. You pick one, or type your own.

Creates the clean script, the original document, and the title on the run.
2 · briefwords

Five decisions about the film: purpose, audience, message, tone, ending. The assistant proposes them from the script and asks only the questions that would change the film. Your answers are stored in your own words. Production details come here too: target length, process stages, brand rules, languages.

Creates the brief with its reasons and your answers.
3 · scriptwords

One wording pass against the brief. Every proposed change is shown as before and after with a reason. You accept all, some, or keep the original. The accepted text is split into numbered beats: a beat is one paragraph of the script, the unit the film is built in (the earlier application called the same thing a passage, P001, P002; BEAT_01 is P001).

Creates the final spoken text with beats BEAT_01, BEAT_02 and so on. Everything after this is bound to this exact text.
4 · voicepaid

Choosing a voice: catalogue browsing, previews, up to six short auditions on one passage, then the full narration. You listen to the files the assistant downloads for you. ElevenLabs is paid; the assistant tells you, then proceeds.

Creates narration.mp3 and a timing file with the start and end of every word. The narration is never cut or changed after this.
5 · mediapictures in

You copy the clips into the run folder. The tool lists them with length, size and sound, skips exact duplicates, converts phone clips with variable frame rate, and uploads each one with a receipt.

Creates the clip list that every later stage refers to.
6 · analysispaid

Each clip is watched once by Gemini 3.8 Flash, which writes a detailed log: usable sub-clips with exact frames, who is talking and when, camera and movement, which beats the clip could serve, clean sound intervals, safety flags. The server then works out, for every sub-clip, whether it can be used as is, cropped to hide a speaker, or not at all. Five clips run at a time.

Creates one log per clip and a 10-line summary card. Ask the assistant for a clip's card at any time.
7 · optionsdeciding the cut

The assistant first shows a coverage report: for each beat, how many good candidates exist and which beats have none. Then it proposes, per beat, a primary and a secondary option, each a short sequence of sub-clips. The server refuses anything that breaks the editing rules. The server renders a review film: a 3-second slate before every numbered option, then the option with its narration.

Creates the review film and the numbered option list. You watch the film.
8 · masterdeciding the cut

You give one decision per option number: keep, reject with a reason, or keep with a small trim. The server checks that nothing is used twice, applies the trims within the rules, and builds a fresh master. Beats with no kept option become gaps for the next stages. Your rejection reasons are remembered for this client's future films.

Creates the master film with gap cards where pictures are still missing, and the list of gaps.
9 · first_cutdeciding the cut

One question: did you watch the master film and listen to it, and do you approve it as the first cut? Your reply must say both.

Creates the approved first cut, locked to the exact film file.
10 · visualspaid

For each gap, the assistant plans what the picture should be, gathers reference images (a frame from a clip, a photo you supply, or a generated image from OpenAI), and either generates a short video take or uses a still or movie you provide. You approve each reference by looking at it, and each take by watching it.

Creates the chosen take or asset for every gap, with its references.
11 · assemblyfinishing

The master plus the gap fills becomes the whole film. You choose per fill how a take sits in its slot, and for any shaky shot whether to leave it, stabilise it, or swap it. The narration is untouched, the clips' own clean sound is placed quietly under it, no music. You watch and listen, ask for changes, or accept.

Creates the complete film as one MP4, with loudness measured and recorded.
12 · deliverymac

The package is written: the approved film, the narration, the original clips used, the normalised fills, the plans, subtitles, a finishing checklist for the editor, and a media-pool sheet. Then the one Mac step: pull the run on the Mac and run the Resolve step once. It builds a DaVinci Resolve 21.1.1 timeline that reproduces the film's cuts and crops, exports it, reopens it to verify, and uploads it. Back here, you confirm five things: watched, listened, read the subtitles, timeline verified, package complete. Then the run closes and keeps only what matters.

Creates the delivery package with Approved-film.mp4 and Approved-timeline.drt, ready for the editor.

Five rules that hold everywhere

  • The server owns the order. The assistant asks "what is next" and does that. It cannot skip, repeat, or go back on its own.
  • Your yes is the record. A stage is accepted only by your explicit reply to the one question. The reply is stored word for word. "ok" or "looks fine" is asked again.
  • Paid steps inform, never ask. The assistant says which provider and proceeds. Costs are recorded per step.
  • Three strikes. A proposal refused three times stops and asks you how to proceed: retry, revise an earlier stage, file a defect, or stop.
  • Anything wrong is a defect, not a debugging session. The assistant files it and stops. The builder reads the server record and fixes the cause.

Where things are

On this machine, everything for one run lives in runs/VW-n/: in/ for what you drop in, out/ for what the assistant and the tool produce, work/ for scratch that is deleted at close. The server keeps every stage's output, your approvals, costs and the files, and the web page at /runs lists every run with its stage.
