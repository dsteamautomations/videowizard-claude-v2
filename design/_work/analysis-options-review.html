Analysis and options: the proposal, checked

A review of the side thread's proposal (changes A to H), today's process against a corrected proposal, two flowcharts, the first paid runs on one clip (section 9), every field the model answers (section 10) and the next test plan (section 11). Nothing here is built into the application.

In short

  • The direction holds: the model that watches the video should judge the fine match, and the result should be measured.
  • The proposal has seven errors and five gaps. Two matter most: the cost figure is about twenty times too low, and moving the pauses breaks the fixed timeline.
  • One change should be dropped as written (sentence fit in the first log) and one redesigned (the second Gemini pass should rank candidates, not only check one).

2. What the proposal missed

#GapWhy it matters
G1Change C only checks the clip the blind placer already chose.It can say "wrong", but it cannot find the better clip the placer never picked. The watcher should be shown the sentence and the best few candidates, and rank them. That is also what you asked to test.
G2The client's library.A clip already in the library is skipped and later matched "by eye, without the model saying which lines it fits" (brandlib.py:178). The first log's script fit is for one film only. So the first pass should describe what is seen, and the second pass should do the fitting. Library clips then get the same check as new ones.
G3The score in change A cannot see a missed clip.The operator answers only the shots that were placed. A blank where good footage existed is never judged. VW-23 has 187 sub-clips and 18 blanks. A second measure is needed: how much narration sits under real footage, and a small audit of blanks.
G4Changes B and C overlap.If the watcher checks every direct match as video, frame strips for the same shots add nothing. Use the video check for direct matches and the strips for B-roll.
G5Load on the server.During VW-23's analysis the database timed out once and the workers retried seven times (08:38 UTC); 8 of 94 jobs needed a second attempt; a job waited 26 minutes on average. More passes add to this. Change E also names no model and cannot judge colour from text.

7. Test frame: Gemini 3.8 Flash against Gemini 4 Hydrogen

For your approval. Nothing runs until you say so. The test runs outside the application and reads copies only; VW-23, the server and the database are not touched.

The two models

  • gemini-3.8-flash: the model in use today.
  • gemini-4-hydrogen: released 12 Nov 2026. Reported price 1.50 dollars per million input tokens and 7.50 per million output. It takes video. Google has not named it as Flash's successor; its documents still list 3.8 Flash as current. I have not confirmed its video limits or its agentic mode from Google's own pages.

Part 1: watch a clip and report

  • Material: six VW-23 clips of different kinds: one clear action, one action in several steps, one with speech, one flagged for risk, one under 15 seconds, one long.
  • Request: the production prompt and answer shape, identical for both models; only the model name differs. Two runs each, to see how much a model disagrees with itself.
  • Truth, written before any output is read: a fact sheet per clip from a person watching it. Eight to ten facts that can be checked: the action, the object, start and end to half a second, people, sound events, and what the clip does not show.
  • Judged blind: the outputs are labelled X and Y. You score them against the fact sheet. No model judges.
  • Measured: facts right, wrong, missing and invented; error at the boundaries in seconds; whether "does not show" is filled and true; whether the answer passes the server's own check; all tokens including video and thinking; seconds; retries.

Part 2: analyse and suggest clips (replaced by section 11)

  • Material: ten sentences from VW-23's approved scenes. Six where footage was placed, two where the operator rejects or trims, two blanks where footage may exist.
  • For each sentence: four candidate sub-clips sent as video. One is a decoy: the right activity, the wrong step.
  • Asked of each model: rank the four; give in and out seconds for the best; does it show exactly this (yes, partly, no); what is missing. "None fits" is an allowed answer.
  • Third arm, no new cost: what Claude placed from text in VW-23. This is today's method and the baseline.
  • Truth: your own pick for each sentence, made by watching the four clips before seeing any model's answer, plus the operator's keep, reject or trim on VW-23.
  • Measured: first choice matching yours; decoys chosen; "none fits" given rightly; in and out within half a second of your trim; tokens and seconds per sentence.
  • Size: one request per sentence with four clips, two models, two runs: forty requests.

Rules set before the run

  • Hydrogen replaces Flash for watching clips only if it has fewer wrong and invented facts on at least five of the six clips.
  • The watcher is worth building only if its first choice matches yours on at least eight of ten sentences and beats the text-only baseline by two or more.
  • One request per model is run first and its full token count reported. You then say go or stop.

Needed from you

  • A Gemini key for the test, given as a file, as with the earlier tests.
  • VW-23's clips and files, copied from the operator's computer or read from storage.
  • About an hour of your time: the fact sheets for six clips and your picks for ten sentences.

What this test will not show

  • One film, one kind of film (a building documentary), small numbers.
  • Gemini as the builder of the whole cut. Part 2 tests the judgement per sentence, not the assembly.
  • Money, until the price of 3.8 Flash and the billing of cached video tokens are confirmed.

What this changes in how a clip is assessed

  • The model already makes editorial judgements that no rule reads. Before adding a new pass, use what is already paid for.
  • Counts and outcomes need care. Hydrogen counted right here, but called a chipped corner a split. Ask for the outcome as "what is different at the end, exactly".
  • Who is speaking is not steady. Treat the speaker label as a lead, not a fact, until tested on more clips.
  • In this test the model was given sentences, not paragraphs, and it fitted each piece to a sentence with a reason and a limit. That is the fine match the proposal wanted from a second pass. It may only need a time range added.

11. Test plan: can Gemini 4 Hydrogen alone say which clip goes where?

For approval. Nothing runs until Deepa says so. It runs outside the application on copies; no run, server or database is touched.

The film

  • VW-19, the short rat-trap bond explainer. It is complete on this computer: 11 sentences in 7 scenes, 46 clips, and Claude's finished cut. No file is needed from another computer.

First, a small check: which way should a short clip be watched?

Added on 13 Nov at Deepa's request. The model can watch a clip in two ways. The plain way looks once. The look-again way lets it go back to parts of the clip. Today the application uses the look-again way only for a clip longer than 15 seconds. That limit is ours, set on 29 Oct; it is not the provider's. No published shortest length for the look-again way was found for any model, and Google's own pages do not name Hydrogen for it. Our one run on a 19-second clip is the only proof here that Hydrogen accepts it.

  • VW-19's clips: 46 in all, from 3.8 to 95 seconds. 28 are 15 seconds or less. 18 are longer.
  • The check: four clips, of 3.8, 8.2, 13.0 and 16.8 seconds. Each is sent to Hydrogen twice, once each way, with the same question as arm 2. Eight small requests.
  • Recorded for each: accepted or refused (an earlier fault was a refusal that came only with the look-again way and the answer form together); how many times it looked; tokens; seconds; and the two answers side by side, for Deepa to say which is better.
  • What it decides: the way each of the 46 clips is sent in arm 2. If the look-again way is refused or no better on short clips, arm 2 does what the application does today: plain for the 28 short clips, look-again for the 18 longer ones. If it is clearly better on short clips, all 46 go the look-again way, and that becomes a proposal to lower the 15-second limit.
