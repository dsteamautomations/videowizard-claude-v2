# Subject rules: rat-trap bond (design/130 seed, 1 Nov 2026)

Each line is one rule a drawing of this subject must keep. The first four are SK's corrections of the shot 2 test (1 Nov 2026); the rest are the masonry truths the test-bench checkers relied on. Operators add rules with `vw motion rule --subject rat-trap-bond --add "…" --quote "…"`.

- The bricks are solid. The cavity is the gap between the two brick faces, shown as one band behind the face (or in section), never as holes inside the bricks.
- A window or door opening has a lintel over it (a solid course or a concrete lintel) bearing on the wall at both sides; bricks never run straight across an opening.
- The wall is built solid (no cavity) at the corners, at junctions with other walls, beside openings (the jambs), and in the courses at sill and lintel level.
- Bricks are bonded across a T-junction or a corner: no vertical joint runs straight through course after course.
- No continuous vertical joint: the joints of each course fall over the middle of the bricks in the course below (stagger every course).
- Rat-trap bond lays every brick on edge: a stretcher shows its long face (230 x 110 mm), a header its end (75 x 110 mm); every course has the same height.
- A rat-trap wall is 230 mm thick, the same as a solid one-brick wall: two leaves of bricks on edge with headers on edge tying them across the cavity.
- The cavity is closed into pockets by the headers in alternate courses; it is not one continuous gap through the wall.
- Brick walls in cement mortar are cured (kept wet) for at least seven days.
